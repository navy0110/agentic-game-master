## Qualify the actual task

Ask these before introducing a detailed sales pitch:

1. When is your next session, and what are you preparing right now?
2. How do you normally prepare it? What takes the most effort?
3. What would make generated material unusable in your campaign?

Record the answers as observations, not inferred pain or assumed time savings.

## Free task — exact prompt

Select the agent in ASI:One, then send:

> Create an NPC for my tabletop campaign. Here is my world: [brief]. Preserve
> this rule: [world rule]. Give the NPC a name, role, motives, a GM-only secret,
> and exactly three lines of dialogue that hint at the secret without
> confessing it. Match this tone: [tone].

Ask the tester to keep the original result and note any correction they make.
Do not coach them through every step: record when assistance was necessary.

## Proposed paid deliverable

**Session Prep Pack — USD 5, one time**

- One short quest with three actionable objectives and possible consequences.
- One NPC with motives, a private GM secret and three dialogue lines.
- One dynamic event with a trigger and consequences.
- Up to two revisions to the same brief.
- Plain-text/Markdown delivery, generated with the agent and reviewed by the team.

This is session preparation, not live GM attendance, rules certification,
game-engine integration, artwork, or an unlimited ongoing campaign service.
Agree on scope and delivery time before any purchase. Do not advertise instant
or same-day fulfillment unless the team can meet that specific deadline.

Before taking money, establish a working checkout/payment method, a delivery
channel, and clear handling of failed delivery. No payment link is supplied here.

## Price-test question — after the free result

> Based on what you just tried, would you buy a USD 5 pack for your next session
> containing one short quest, a complete NPC, an event, and up to two revisions,
> reviewed by our team? What would stop you from buying it?

A yes is stated interest, not a sale. Record payment only after it is received,
and record delivery separately. Do not treat a hackathon rating as purchase intent.

## Follow up after the scheduled session

> Which part did you actually use in your session?
> What did you change before using it?
> Did it reduce preparation effort? Please compare it with how you normally work.
> Would you use it for your next session? What would you ask it to prepare?

Obtain permission before publishing a quote or screenshot. Keep raw contact
details, payment identifiers and private conversation links outside the public repo.

## Decide what to do next

For the first five GMs, aim to learn whether at least three use the content in a
real session, two return, and one buys a delivered pilot. These are exploratory
targets, not measured traction or statistical proof. If people like the idea but
do not use the output, fix the task/value proposition before building billing.

Measure manual review time. A low-priced concierge pilot may validate demand
without being profitable; do not describe that as a scalable margin until the
actual fulfillment costs support it.

Hackathon evidence remains separate: qualifying assigned Trick or Feedback
review through ASI:One plus the documented improvement. A direct community
recruit can be a real customer without being a qualifying assigned reviewer.
